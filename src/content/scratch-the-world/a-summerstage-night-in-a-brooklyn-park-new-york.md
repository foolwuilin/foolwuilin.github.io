Not every stop on Scratch the World is me behind a turntable. Some of them are just me in the crowd, listening. Pin number three took me across the East River to Brooklyn on a hot July evening in 2017, to a free CityParks SummerStage show in the park — where the boom-bap was loud, the light was going gold, and the whole borough seemed to be singing back at the stage. I'm DJ FOOL Wuilin, and this one was about soaking it in.

## A free show in a Brooklyn park

Every summer, CityParks SummerStage carries free concerts into parks all over New York — a big season at Central Park's Rumsey Playfield, and neighbourhood shows scattered across the other boroughs, including plenty in Brooklyn. This was one of those nights: no ticket, no barrier, just a stage dropped into a park and a crowd packed in shoulder to shoulder in front of it. I only caught a few seconds of video, hands and phones in the air, because that's the kind of set where you'd rather feel it than film it. "Turn it up, turn it up" pretty much summed up the mood.

## A city still carrying Prodigy

The timing gave the whole evening its weight. Prodigy of Mobb Deep had passed away on 20 June 2017, and two weeks later New York was still holding it — his verses pouring out of passing cars and bodega doorways all over town. Mobb Deep repped Queensbridge, but Brooklyn claimed him just the same, and a boom-bap show in that exact moment felt less like a concert and more like a big, sweaty group exhale. You could hear people put a little extra into every line.

## Why Brooklyn, and why these parks

If New York gave hip-hop its start, Brooklyn's parks are where a lot of it grew up. Shows and cyphers here have long run through places like Herbert Von King Park in Bed-Stuy — the old Tompkins Park, with its amphitheatre ringed by 19th-century brownstones — Fort Greene Park, laid out by Olmsted and Vaux beneath the Prison Ship Martyrs' Monument, and Commodore Barry Park, Brooklyn's oldest, home of the Brooklyn Hip-Hop Festival. This is the ground that raised The Notorious B.I.G., Big Daddy Kane and Jay-Z, and you feel that lineage in the little things: the G train rumbling under the pavement, rows of ornate brownstones down MacDonough and Macon, the landmarked old Magnolia Grandiflora tree keeping watch over Lafayette Avenue.

## The DJ Premier sound, outdoors

The tag on my clip was #djpremier, and that tells you the sound of the night. Premier — one half of Gang Starr with Guru — is the architect of East Coast boom-bap: the gritty, chopped-up, heavy-drum records he built at the legendary D&D Studios defined an era for Biggie, Jeru the Damaja and Group Home. Hearing those drums outdoors, bouncing off the trees and a few thousand people, is a completely different thing from hearing them in headphones. Outside, the low end lives in your chest.

## What that night felt like

A Brooklyn park in July is thick, humid and alive. Under the music there was the slap of dominoes on folding tables, the squeak of sneakers on a hot asphalt court, the metallic crack of a handball, and the warbling jingle of a Mister Softee truck circling the edges. The air was sweet with smoke and the charcoal smell of jerk chicken off an oil-drum grill, while someone worked the crowd selling frozen "Nutcrackers" out of a deli bag. The sun dropped behind the big London plane and sycamore trees, the stage lights came up, and everyone's hands went with them.

## Why a crowd night belongs on this map

Scratch the World is about carrying this music into ordinary places and paying attention. Most days that's me on a park bench with a portable turntable; some days, like this one, it's just standing in a Brooklyn crowd letting a Premier beat hit me in the chest. Both feed the same thing. Manhattan is where the idea sparked, and Union Square is where it became a habit — but this Brooklyn night was a good reminder of what all of it is actually for.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is CityParks SummerStage?

It's New York's big free outdoor performing-arts festival, run by the City Parks Foundation. The main season happens at Central Park's Rumsey Playfield, with additional free "neighbourhood" shows staged in parks across all five boroughs each summer — spanning hip-hop, jazz, global sounds, dance and more.

### Where does SummerStage happen in Brooklyn?

Beyond the Central Park main stage, SummerStage brings free neighbourhood concerts to Brooklyn parks such as Herbert Von King Park in Bed-Stuy, plus other green spaces around the borough. The exact line-up and venues change from summer to summer.

### Why does a concert night fit a scratch DJ's travel diary?

Because Scratch the World was never only about the scratching — it's a hip-hop journey through the places the music lives. Standing in the crowd for a boom-bap show in Brooklyn, days after losing Prodigy, is as much a part of that story as any turntable session.

### Who was Prodigy, and why the tribute mood?

Prodigy was one half of the Queensbridge duo Mobb Deep, and he passed away on 20 June 2017. Two weeks later, in early July, New York was still very much in mourning — which is what gave this Brooklyn boom-bap show its heavy, communal feeling.
