Stop four on Scratch the World was the first one I didn't organise myself. At the end of July 2017 the Rock Steady Crew — the crew that has carried breaking since 1977 — held its 40th Anniversary celebration at Rumsey Playfield in Central Park, and I ended up in a cypher on the grass with a portable turntable balanced on my bag. I'm DJ FOOL Wuilin, and this was the afternoon a bag-sized rig stopped being a private hobby and became a way into a room.

## Thanks for having me

I want to be straight about this one: it wasn't my show. I got waved in. The Open Faders crew in New York has spent years building a community around DJs who carry their setups instead of loading them into a van, and that weekend they brought portables to the Rock Steady anniversary. I was the guy from Taiwan with a Numark PT01 in a shoulder bag. Someone opened a gap in the circle, someone else counted me in, and I took my turn over a beat from *All-City Battle Breaks* that wouldn't even be released until September. Four weeks earlier I'd been sitting on a lawn in Union Square with one friend. This was a very different kind of afternoon.

## Where Rumsey Playfield actually is

If you've never been, Rumsey Playfield is easy to miss on a map. It's a fenced performance ground tucked mid-park on Central Park's East Side, roughly between East 69th and East 72nd Streets. The quickest way in is off Fifth Avenue at 69th Street through Inventors' Gate, then west along a path that curves gently downhill toward the gates. It sits just east of the Naumburg Bandshell and the top of the Mall, with Bethesda Terrace and the Lake a short walk to the northwest. Inside the fence everything is levelled and surfaced for staging and heavy foot traffic. Outside it, the ground goes straight back to being Central Park — packed dirt, exposed roots, low rustic post-and-rail fencing and grey outcrops of Manhattan schist under American elms, pin oaks and London planes. That outside part is the part I care about, because that's where you can actually sit down and set up.

## Forty years, one afternoon

The Rock Steady anniversary isn't a concert with a hip-hop theme. It's closer to a family reunion that the whole world flies in for. On 30 July 2017 the crowd at Rumsey ran from original b-boys who were there in the seventies to breaking crews who'd come in from Japan, France and South Korea, plus writers in painted denim and neighbourhood families with strollers. That year it also doubled as a tribute to Prodigy of Mobb Deep, who had died five weeks earlier — Havoc, DJ Premier and MC Lyte all played. You could feel it in the field. Every so often the whole crowd would go up at once for a record that meant something to every single person standing in it.

## The cypher outside the gates

The thing I want people to understand about that day is that the best stuff wasn't on the stage. Cyphers kept breaking out along the perimeter and on the pavement over by the Bandshell — tight circles maybe fifteen or twenty feet across, shoulder to shoulder, dancers rolling out taped-down linoleum or just spinning straight on the smooth stone. Our corner of it was four of us and a couple of portables propped on a bag: @dspliff139, @djlokash, @djrolirho and me, trading turns on the fader while people leaned in over each other's shoulders. Nobody announces anything at a cypher. You watch the circle, you wait for the gap, and you go.

## What a battery rig sounds like under the elms

Scratching outdoors in Central Park in late July is a specific kind of work. The canopy gives you hard-edged dappled shade — blinding white where the sun gets through, deep green-gold everywhere else — and the air is thick with sun-baked asphalt, halal cart smoke drifting in off Fifth Avenue, sunscreen and crushed grass. Underneath it all there's a permanent bed of noise: bike tyres and skates humming along East Drive, carriage horses, sirens sliding down Fifth, an acoustic busker somewhere up the Mall. And when the main line arrays inside the fence fired, the low end came up through the ground and into your arms and rattled the leaves overhead. A PT01 running on batteries is never going to win that fight, so you don't pick it. You sit inside the noise, keep the cuts tight and short, and let the park be your low end.

## Playing in Central Park, honestly

People ask me whether any of this is even allowed, so here's the honest version. Unamplified playing in New York City parks is free and protected; the moment you add an amp or a powered speaker you are technically in permit territory — an NYPD sound device permit, and a Parks permit for anything that counts as an event. There are hard quiet zones where amplified sound gets shut down quickly: Sheep Meadow, Strawberry Fields, the Ramble and Conservatory Water. Around the Mall, the Bandshell and the edges of Rumsey during a jam, a small battery rig at conversational volume is completely normal and nobody minds. The unwritten rule is the same one I follow everywhere I've played: don't block the path, don't distort, don't hustle anyone for money. Keep it at that level in New York and heads will nod. Cross it and someone will flag a Parks officer before your second record.

## Why this pin matters

Union Square taught me that a turntable in a bag turns any patch of grass into a session. Central Park taught me the other half of it: the same bag gets you into rooms you'd never otherwise be invited to. I turned up as a visitor with no crew and no booking, and I left having played in a cypher at the fortieth anniversary of the crew that started all of this. That's the entire argument for portablism, compressed into one hot afternoon.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is Rumsey Playfield in Central Park?

Rumsey Playfield is the fenced performance ground mid-park on Central Park's East Side, roughly between East 69th and East 72nd Streets. Enter from Fifth Avenue at 69th Street (Inventors' Gate) and follow the path west and slightly downhill. It sits just east of the Naumburg Bandshell and the north end of the Mall, and southeast of Bethesda Terrace. It's the home of Central Park SummerStage.

### What is the Rock Steady Crew Anniversary jam?

It's the annual celebration of the Rock Steady Crew, the breaking crew founded in the Bronx in 1977, and one of the longest-running gatherings in hip-hop. The 40th anniversary edition took place at Rumsey Playfield in Central Park on 30 July 2017 and drew b-boys and b-girls, DJs and writers from around the world. That year it also served as a tribute to Prodigy of Mobb Deep, with Havoc, DJ Premier and MC Lyte performing.

### What gear do you need to scratch in a park?

Very little. A battery-powered portable turntable such as the Numark PT01 Scratch, a scratch record, and optionally a small portable fader and speaker. Because it runs on batteries there's no outlet to find, and the whole rig fits in a shoulder bag — which is exactly what makes sessions like this one possible.

### Can you DJ or play music in Central Park?

Acoustic, unamplified performance in New York City parks is permit-free. Amplified sound generally is not: it requires an NYPD sound device permit, and larger setups need a Parks permit as well. Amplification is actively enforced against in Central Park's designated quiet zones — Sheep Meadow, Strawberry Fields, the Ramble and Conservatory Water. A small battery rig kept at low volume near the Mall or the Bandshell is usually fine, as long as you keep the pathway clear and the volume conversational.
