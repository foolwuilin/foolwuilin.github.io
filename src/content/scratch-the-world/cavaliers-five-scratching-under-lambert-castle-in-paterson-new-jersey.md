On 22 October 2017, five of us hauled battery-powered Numark PT01 Scratch turntables up the hill to Lambert Castle in Paterson, New Jersey, lined them up in front of the old stone walls, and passed cuts down the line like a relay team. My caption that day called us the "Cavaliers Five": guarding under the castle, cutting on the fortress. It was a joke, but standing on that hillside in the October sun, it didn't feel far off.

## A castle built on silk money

Lambert Castle isn't a fairy-tale prop. It was built in 1892 as "Belle Vista", the home of Catholina Lambert, an English-born silk manufacturer who made his fortune when Paterson was known as "Silk City". He built it out of stone quarried from the mountain it sits on, and you can feel that in person: the walls are rough, heavy and warm-coloured, with arches, battlements and a tower that make it look like it was dropped in from another century. Today the building is home to the Passaic County Historical Society, so it's a museum and a landmark, not just a backdrop.

I'd seen photos before, but the first thing that hit me when we walked up was how solid it all feels. You set a plastic portable turntable on a ledge built from quarried mountain stone, and suddenly your gear feels very small and very new.

## The view from Garret Mountain

The castle sits on the eastern slope of Garret Mountain, part of the Watchung ridge that rises right out of Paterson. From the terraces you look straight down over the city's rooftops and old mill district, and on a clear day the Manhattan skyline shows up on the horizon, a thin grey line of towers. For a crew that spent most weekends scratching in New York parks, it was a funny feeling: we'd driven out of the city to look back at it.

Up the hill is Garret Mountain Reservation, with its stone observation tower and Barbour's Pond. Down in the valley are the Great Falls of the Passaic, the waterfall that powered Paterson's mills in the first place. Valley Road runs along the bottom of the castle grounds, and you can hear it faintly the whole time.

## Late October on the hill

We couldn't have picked a better week. The maples and oaks around the grounds had turned rust, orange and gold, and the low autumn sun lit up the stone so it almost glowed. The shade was cold and the sunny patches of wall were warm to the touch, so everyone kept drifting toward the light with their decks.

The soundtrack was wind coming up the ridge, dry leaves skittering across the lawn, a low hum of traffic from the city below, and then our five small speakers ticking and chirping over the top of it. It was a Sunday, so there were families walking, people taking photos by the arches, and a few curious visitors who stopped to figure out what five people with tiny record players were doing outside a castle.

## Five portables, one routine

The whole point of the day was playing together. The caption lists the order: @turtlescratch, then @_justdj_, then me, then @talamarc, then @dj_avana. We ran it like a tag-team routine, where each person takes a short phrase and hands the beat down the line to the next deck. With portables, nobody is "on the decks" and everybody else waiting; you're all sitting in a row, each with your own turntable, listening and answering.

On the PT01 you don't have a mixer channel to hide behind. The built-in speaker is small, so you lean in, and everyone can hear exactly what you just did. That makes it honest and a lot of fun. Someone throws a quick chirp, the next person answers with a flare, and by the fifth deck the pattern has turned into something none of us planned.

## How I'd set up at a historic site like this

Lambert Castle is a museum and a county park, not a stage, so we kept it low-key:

- **Batteries only.** There are no outlets on the grounds, and you don't want to go looking for one. The PT01 runs on batteries, which is exactly why it's perfect for spots like this.
- **Use the walls and lawns, not the doorways.** We set up along the stone ledges and grass, away from the museum entrance and paths, so visitors could walk by easily.
- **Keep it at conversation volume.** Five small speakers are plenty. The goal is for the people next to you to hear it, not the whole hillside or the inside of the museum.
- **Share the space with the photographers.** The arches and terraces are popular for photos. If someone's shooting, shift over and give them the shot.
- **Carry out everything.** Spare batteries, cables, slipmats, record sleeves. Leave the stone exactly how you found it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Lambert Castle?

It's a stone mansion built in 1892 as "Belle Vista" for Catholina Lambert, a Paterson silk-mill owner. It stands on the slope of Garret Mountain above Paterson, New Jersey, and is now home to the Passaic County Historical Society's museum.

### Can you see New York City from Lambert Castle?

Yes. From the terraces you look east over Paterson, and on a clear day the Manhattan skyline appears on the horizon. The views get even wider from the observation tower up in Garret Mountain Reservation.

### Can you play music at Lambert Castle?

It's a historic site in a county park, so this isn't a place for a busking set with an amp. A small, battery-powered, low-volume session like ours, set up away from the museum entrance and paths, felt respectful. If staff or park rangers ask you to stop, pack up right away.

### What gear did you use?

A battery-powered Numark PT01 Scratch portable turntable, a record, and headphones. The other four DJs brought their own portables, so it was five decks in a row.
