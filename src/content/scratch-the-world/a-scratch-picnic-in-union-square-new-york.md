Stop two on Scratch the World is my favourite kind of session: no stage, no plan, just a patch of grass and a turntable. A week after that very first clip in Manhattan, my friend Marc — you might know him as @talamarc — and I met up at Union Square Park with a Numark PT01 and one loose idea: a scratch picnic. We found a shady spot up by the north-end dog run, spread everything out on the lawn, and spent a July afternoon cutting records while all of downtown New York moved around us. I'm DJ FOOL Wuilin, and here's how the second pin on the map went down.

## A scratch picnic, not a gig

There was nothing official about this one. We weren't set up to perform for anyone — we just wanted to sit in the park and scratch. Marc and I dropped our bags on the grass, propped the turntable on top, and got comfortable like anyone else out for a lazy summer afternoon. That's the whole spirit of a scratch picnic: you bring the same gear you'd practise with at home, except home is now a lawn in the middle of Manhattan, and the soundtrack you're cutting over is an entire city. Half the fun was how normal it felt — two friends, one turntable, and a few hours to kill.

## Why Union Square is a natural stage

If you've spent any time in New York, you know Union Square is one of its great crossroads. Several subway lines meet right underneath it, so there's a constant churn of people cutting through — students from the nearby universities, commuters, activists with signs, chess players hunched over their boards, and tourists drifting off Broadway. On its south side you'll find the famous wide steps and the old equestrian statue of George Washington, and on Greenmarket days the north and west edges fill up with farm stands. It's loud, green, and completely used to people doing their own thing in public — which is exactly why nobody blinks when you unpack a turntable.

## The north-end dog run

We set up near the dog run at the northern end of the park. It's a fenced gravel pocket, shaded by the big London plane trees that line the paths, and it's a little calmer than the busy southern steps — a nice place to actually hear what you're doing. Dogs sprint around behind the fence, owners chat on the benches, and the tree canopy keeps the worst of the July sun off your records. For a relaxed session where you're sitting on the grass rather than working a crowd, that leafy north end is hard to beat.

## The little rig on the lawn

The whole setup fit in a bag: a **Numark PT01 Scratch** — a battery-powered portable turntable built for scratch DJs — sitting right on top of it. No power outlet, no mixer stand, no booth. Where the Manhattan session the week before was all about testing a wireless Mixfader, this one was stripped back on purpose: just the PT01, a record, and the two of us trading turns on the fader. That portability is the entire reason Scratch the World is even possible. If it doesn't fit in a bag I can sling over my shoulder on the subway, it doesn't come.

## What it actually feels like out there

Scratching in a park is its own thing. Under everything you can feel the low rumble of the subway through the ground, the traffic drone off the surrounding avenues, dogs barking, kids running past — and you learn to lock your rhythm in and let all of it become the backing track. Every so often someone slows down, tilts their head, and you catch the little double-take of a person realising that's a real record being cut on the grass. Nobody makes a big deal of it. That's New York: the city has seen everything, so you're free to just play.

## Why these small sessions matter

Looking back, this laid-back picnic set the tone for everything that came after. Scratch the World was never meant to be about big stages — it's about carrying a turntable into ordinary places and leaving a little sound behind. A park bench, a dog run, a patch of shade with a friend: that's the heart of it. Manhattan was where the idea sparked, but Union Square was where it started to feel like a habit worth keeping.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the Union Square dog run?

It's at the northern end of Union Square Park in Manhattan, New York — a fenced gravel run shaded by the park's London plane trees, a short walk up from the wide southern steps and the George Washington statue. Several subway lines stop right underneath the square, so it's easy to reach.

### What is a scratch picnic?

A scratch picnic is exactly what it sounds like: taking a portable turntable to a park, sitting on the grass, and scratching for fun instead of performing on a stage. It's the most relaxed form of portablism — the same gear you'd practise with at home, brought outdoors with a friend.

### What gear do you need to scratch in a park?

Not much. A battery-powered portable turntable like the Numark PT01 Scratch, a scratch record, and optionally a small portable fader or speaker. Because it runs on batteries, you don't need a power outlet — the whole rig fits in a shoulder bag.

### Is it okay to scratch in a public park like Union Square?

Casual, low-volume playing in a big public space like Union Square is generally fine and very common — the park is full of musicians and performers on any given day. If you plan to amplify loudly, draw a crowd, or set up a proper performance, that can fall under New York City's street-performance and permit rules, so keep it mellow and courteous if you're just there for a picnic session.
