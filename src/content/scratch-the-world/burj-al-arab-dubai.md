Can I tell you about the time I scratched in front of the Burj Al Arab? I was in Dubai in July, and instead of just snapping the same photo everyone takes of that sail-shaped hotel, I pulled out my little portable turntable and cut a few bars right there with it behind me. If you're heading to Dubai and you're even a little into music, I think you'd love doing exactly this. Here's how it went — and how you can do it too.

## Why I was in Dubai in the first place

Quick backstory, because people always ask: I studied at Hult International Business School, and one of the nicest perks of being a Hult alum is that you can go back and take a course at any of their campuses each year. So I turned a study trip to the Dubai campus into a proper little vacation — and the one thing at the top of my bucket list was seeing the Burj Al Arab up close. If you ever get the chance to mix a bit of learning with travel like this, take it. The best trips usually have a reason behind them.

## The best spot to see the Burj Al Arab (and it's free)

Here's the tip I wish someone had given me: you don't need a fancy hotel booking to get a great view of the Burj Al Arab. Head to **Madinat Jumeirah** — it's a resort district with a traditional souk (market), winding canals, and little abra boats, and it's completely free to wander. From the walkways and terraces you get this gorgeous, postcard-perfect view of the Burj Al Arab framed by Arabian-style architecture. It honestly looks like an old Middle Eastern street, but with beautiful resorts and great restaurants tucked all around. That's where I set up.

## What it actually feels like out there

You always catch a bit of a chill in Dubai — and I mean that in the best way. Even in the middle of July, the sea breeze rolling in keeps you so calm that you barely notice the heat. Scratching there, with that breeze going and the Burj Al Arab glowing in the background, felt unreal — peaceful and a little surreal at the same time. If you've only ever pictured Dubai as blazing hot, this spot by the water might surprise you.

## My travel rig (the same one, eight years strong)

The best part is how little you need. I brought the exact same setup I've used everywhere: a **Numark PT01 Scratch** — a battery-powered portable turntable made for scratch DJs, so you don't need to find a power outlet — with a **Mini Innofader** dropped in to replace the stock switch, so I can do real fader cuts. Here's the kicker: this rig has been going strong for eight years and still works perfectly. You really don't need the newest or most expensive gear to make memories like this. One good little setup can travel the world with you.

## Is it okay to do this in Dubai? Honestly, yes

If setting up in public in a foreign country sounds nerve-wracking, I get it — but Dubai made it easy. The people are lovely, the whole place feels calm and orderly, and there's very little to worry about. It's one of those spots where you just feel safe doing your thing. I never felt out of place or on edge. So if that's the thing holding you back, don't let it — this is about as friendly a place as you'll find to try something a little bold.

## Why I scratch the world (and why you might too)

This is what "Scratch the World" is all about. Most people visit the Burj Al Arab and leave with a photo; I like to leave with a scratch. Dubai will always sound, in my memory, like a warm sea breeze, a calm evening, and a few clean cuts in front of one of the most famous buildings on earth. Pick a place that means something to you, bring whatever gear you've got, and go make your own version.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the best place to see the Burj Al Arab for free?

Madinat Jumeirah is one of the best free spots. It's a resort and souk district with canals and traditional Arabian architecture, and its walkways and terraces give you a beautifully framed view of the Burj Al Arab. You can wander it for free, and there are plenty of restaurants and cafés if you want to sit with the view.

### What gear did you use to scratch in Dubai?

The same travel kit I always use: a Numark PT01 Scratch — a battery-powered portable turntable built for scratch DJs — with an Audio Innovate Mini Innofader swapped in for real fader cuts. No booth, no mixer, no power outlet needed. It fits in a bag and has lasted me eight years.

### What is portablism?

Portablism is scratching with a small portable turntable instead of full-size decks. It shrinks a DJ setup down to a little turntable and a fader you can carry anywhere — which is exactly why you can set up and scratch at a beach, a souk, or in front of the Burj Al Arab.

### Is Dubai safe for tourists doing things like this?

In my experience, very. Dubai feels calm, friendly, and orderly, with very little to worry about as a visitor. I felt completely safe and welcome setting up in public around Madinat Jumeirah, and never once felt out of place.
