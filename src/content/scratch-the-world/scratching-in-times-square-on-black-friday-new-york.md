On Black Friday, 25 November 2017, I carried a battery-powered Numark PT01 Scratch into Times Square and set it down in the middle of the holiday-shopping crush. My caption that day was short and a little smug: "Black Friday!! You go shopping. I go cutting." While half of New York was fighting over doorbusters, I plugged in a Mixfader and cut a few bars under the biggest screens in the city.

## The busiest crossroads in New York

Times Square isn't really a square — it's a bowtie, the slanted X where Broadway cuts diagonally across Seventh Avenue between 42nd and 47th Streets in Midtown Manhattan. Since the middle of it was pedestrianised, the centre of that bowtie is car-free: long stretches of granite paving, rows of moveable metal café chairs, and low granite benches you can actually set a turntable down on. At the north end, around 47th Street, the red glass TKTS steps rise over Father Duffy Square like a little amphitheatre, with the half-price Broadway ticket booth tucked underneath and tourists packed on the stairs looking south over the whole glowing canyon. That view south is the postcard everyone photographs — and on Black Friday afternoon it was the backdrop over my shoulder while I cut.

## Scratching under a wall of light

The thing nobody warns you about is how bright it is, even in the middle of the afternoon. The buildings here are wrapped in multi-storey LED screens — One Times Square, where the New Year's Eve ball drops, the slab-sided Marriott Marquis, the giant curved displays throwing out Coca-Cola red, Samsung blue and ABC Studios colour. They're so intense that they wash the daylight out and lay a shifting glow of magenta and cyan and electric blue right across the granite. You feel slightly ridiculous setting up a little turntable under all of that, like busking inside a pinball machine. I loved it. There is no stage anywhere in the world that lights itself for you like Times Square does.

## A Black Friday afternoon in the cold

Late November in New York bites. It was somewhere around the high 30s to mid 40s Fahrenheit, and the wind funnelled straight down the skyscraper canyons and across the open plaza, so my fingers went stiff on the fader inside half an hour. And the crowd — Black Friday afternoon is wall-to-wall gridlock, a slow river of shoppers hauling bulging bags, out-of-town families, and locals cutting through on their way somewhere else. The soundtrack is pure New York: sirens, cabs leaning on their horns while stuck on the cross streets, and the hiss of the halal and street-meat carts, so the whole place smells of roasting lamb and onions, candied nuts, soft pretzels and bus exhaust, all sharpened by the freezing air. Scratching in that isn't about hearing yourself cleanly; it's about finding a pocket in the city's own noise.

## Where a performer is actually allowed to stand

Times Square already runs on a whole ecosystem of hustlers — costumed characters like Spider-Man, Elmo and the Naked Cowboy, rappers pressing CDs into your hands, musicians and buskers. What a lot of visitors don't notice is that it's all quietly zoned. Back in 2016 the city painted teal-coloured rectangles onto the plaza — Designated Activity Zones, each roughly eight by fifty feet — and if you're performing or soliciting tips you're supposed to stay inside those boxes, clear of the marked pedestrian flow lanes that keep the crowd moving. Step outside the teal and you invite an NYPD conversation. So I did what you do: found a box, stayed in it, and kept out of everyone's walking path.

## Playing a portable turntable in the middle of it all

The practical beauty of the Numark PT01 is that it runs on batteries, so there's no banned gas generator humming away and nothing to plug in. The moment you add an external speaker or amp in a New York public space, though, you're technically into NYPD Sound Device Permit territory — historically a roughly $45 application filed a few days ahead at the local precinct. I kept it legal and simple by playing on the deck's own tiny built-in speaker, which Times Square's roar swallows whole unless people lean right in. That turns out to be the fun of it: instead of broadcasting at anyone, you pull a little huddle of curious strangers out of the Black Friday river, all crowding in close to work out what the kid with the turntable is doing. With NYPD foot patrols and heavily armed counter-terror units parked all over the plaza on a holiday weekend, staying inside the teal box, keeping the walking lanes clear and being ready to pack up with a smile is just good manners — and good survival.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where did you scratch on 25 November 2017?

In Times Square, in Midtown Manhattan — on the pedestrian plazas along Broadway between roughly 42nd and 47th Streets, near the red TKTS steps at Father Duffy Square, with the big LED billboards overhead.

### Do you need a permit to scratch in Times Square?

Playing a small battery-powered turntable on its own built-in speaker generally doesn't require one, but the moment you add an external loudspeaker or amplifier you need an NYPD Sound Device Permit (historically about $45, filed in advance at the precinct). On top of that, if you're performing for tips you're meant to stay inside the teal "activity zone" boxes painted on the plaza. Always check the current NYC rules before you go.

### Isn't Times Square far too loud to scratch in?

Honestly, yes — the traffic, sirens and sheer scale of the place drown out a tiny portable deck completely. But that's part of the charm. People have to come right up to hear it, so instead of a stage you end up with a tight little huddle of curious strangers, which is a much better way to meet New York anyway.

### What gear did you use?

A Numark PT01 Scratch — a portable, battery-powered turntable — paired with a wireless Mixfader. The whole rig fits in a shoulder bag and sets up on any bench or ledge in seconds.
