**Quick answer:** On November 12, 2017, a sudden cold snap hit New York, and I still carried my Numark PT01 Scratch into Central Park to cut a few bars among the bare elms. Portable turntables, cold fingers and a winter sky: that is the whole story.

## The day the temperature fell off a cliff

The caption I wrote on Instagram says it all: *freezing!* The temperature was dropping so fast that afternoon that I could feel it between one cut and the next. Mid-November is exactly when New York flips from autumn to early winter, and Central Park shows it first. The canopy thins out almost overnight, the humidity turns brittle, and the light goes a crisp, steel blue. I had a turntable, a Mixfader and a bag that fit on my shoulder, so I told myself the cold was just another sound I had to scratch against.

## Why Central Park in November feels different

Central Park is 843 acres of designed landscape, and in summer it is packed. In November it belongs to the locals: bundled runners, dog walkers in down jackets, and a few visitors huddled around the famous spots. Near Columbus Circle at the southwest corner, the subway rumble and glass towers give way to damp asphalt and the muffled clop of carriage horses. Further in, The Mall runs from 66th to 72nd Street under a roof of American elms, and by mid-November the last amber leaves are sitting on the stone pavers. The sun gets low early, so the shadows are already long by about 3 PM. Honestly, the park sounds different too: with the summer crowds gone, the city roar fades into a soft white noise and you can hear every footstep and every leaf.

## Setting up a portable rig in the cold

My setup was the same as always: a Numark PT01 Scratch (battery-powered, so no outlet needed), a Mixfader for the fader work, and a record to cut. The cold changes a few things. Your fingers stiffen and your timing goes first, so I kept my warm-ups slow and made the baby scratch my main move. Cold hands also make the record feel different under your fingertips, and the pavers and iron benches around you are freezing to the touch. I also kept the session short. Chestnut and honey-nut carts near the park edges perfume the air with sweet smoke, and standing near that smell was the best heater I had.

## Street music in Central Park, and the etiquette

Central Park has a long street-performance tradition. You'll find string players in the arcade under Bethesda Terrace, whose tiled ceiling gives it a famous echo, and singers and guitarists around Strawberry Fields at West 72nd Street, in front of the Dakota building and the black-and-white "Imagine" mosaic. The unwritten rule is to give other performers space and keep your sound from running over theirs. On the legal side, my understanding from my research is that unamplified playing is generally fine, while amplified sound usually needs a permit from NYC Parks and a sound-device permit from the NYPD. Rules change, so please check with NYC Parks before you set up a speaker. I kept my volume low and the session brief.

## What I took away from it

Scratching in the cold taught me patience. Warm-up matters, short sessions beat stubborn ones, and a place you love looks completely different in another season. Central Park in a cold November is quieter, sharper and a little lonelier than the postcard version, and that suits a turntable. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can you scratch outdoors in cold weather?

Yes, but warm up slowly. Cold fingers lose timing and control, so start with simple baby scratches, keep sessions short, and wear thin gloves or keep your hands warm between takes. A battery-powered turntable like the Numark PT01 Scratch also needs a charged battery, since cold drains batteries faster.

### Do you need a permit to play music in Central Park?

From what I have gathered, unamplified performance is generally allowed, while amplified sound usually requires permits from NYC Parks and the NYPD. Rules can change, so confirm with NYC Parks before you bring a speaker.

### Where do musicians play in Central Park?

Popular spots include the Bethesda Terrace arcade, the Mall's elm-lined promenade and the area around Strawberry Fields near West 72nd Street. Each has its own crowd and its own acoustics.

### What is portablism?

Portablism is scratching with a small, portable turntable instead of full-size decks, so you can set up anywhere from a park path to a street corner.
