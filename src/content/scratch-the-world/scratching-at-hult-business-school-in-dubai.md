"Hello Dubai — I'm back." That's honestly how this stop felt. I'd been to the city before for the big postcard spots, but this time I wanted to plant a pin somewhere quieter and more personal: a business-school campus in the middle of Dubai's tech quarter. So I packed my little Numark PT01, walked straight into the July heat, and cut a few bars at Hult International Business School. I'm DJ FOOL Wuilin (吳奕霖), and here's how scratching on a Dubai campus actually went.

## Back in Dubai, this time somewhere new

My first trips to Dubai were all about the icons — the Burj Al Arab sail on the horizon, the giant malls, the Marina. Beautiful, but everyone photographs those. This time I wanted the version of the city that locals actually move through every day, so I headed inland to Dubai Knowledge Park and set up outside Hult International Business School. No crowd, no skyline money-shot — just a campus, a table, and a turntable. It felt less like a tourist stop and more like dropping in on the city's working, studying heart.

## Where Hult sits — Dubai's tech and knowledge quarter

Hult's Dubai campus is in the Injaz Building in Dubai Knowledge Park, right next to Dubai Internet City and the Barsha Heights (TECOM) district. If you've never been, picture wide palm-lined boulevards, clean glass mid-rise towers, and the regional offices of half the tech world — Google, Microsoft, LinkedIn, Meta — all within a short walk. It's calm and orderly in a way that surprises first-time visitors: less neon spectacle, more quiet campus energy. That contrast is exactly why I liked it as a scratch spot. A turntable sounds different against glass and palm trees than it does against Times Square neon.

## Beating the Dubai July heat

I won't pretend July in Dubai is gentle — it routinely pushes past 40°C, and the middle of the day feels like standing in front of a hairdryer. The trick is timing and shade. I set up in the softer part of the day, kept the gear out of direct sun, and leaned on the covered walkways the campus and surrounding towers give you. Every indoor space is fiercely air-conditioned, so you're never far from a cool-down, and a faint breeze drifts in from the coast a few kilometres away. Respect the heat, plan around it, and Dubai is a genuinely comfortable place to play outdoors.

## The little rig that travels with me

The setup here was the same one I carry everywhere: a Numark PT01 Scratch — a battery-powered portable turntable built for scratch DJs, so I'm never hunting for a power outlet — paired with a Mixfader, the wireless Bluetooth crossfader that gives me real, responsive cuts on the road. Add a small speaker so anyone walking past can actually hear it, and that's the whole kit. It all fits in a bag I can sling over one shoulder and carry through an airport, onto a metro, or into a campus courtyard. That portability is the entire reason Scratch the World exists.

## What it feels like to scratch on a campus

Scratching at a business school is a different energy from a street corner. There's a focused, purposeful hum — students crossing between buildings, professionals on calls, the occasional double-take when someone realises the sound is coming from a real spinning record. People here were curious and friendly; a few slowed down, a couple asked what the little turntable was, and nobody minded the noise. After the sensory overload of the Marina and the malls, cutting a few bars in that calm, modern setting felt almost meditative — just me, the PT01, and the Dubai afternoon.

## Why I keep scratching the world

Every stop on this map teaches me something, and Hult reminded me that a place doesn't need to be famous to be worth a pin. A campus in a tech park isn't a tourist landmark, but it's real Dubai — the version where people build, study, and get on with their lives. Leaving a scratch there felt like leaving a small, friendly signature on the working city. Pick a place that means something to you, bring whatever gear you've got, and go add your own pin to the map.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is Hult International Business School in Dubai?

Hult's Dubai campus is in the Injaz Building in Dubai Knowledge Park, directly next to Dubai Internet City in the Barsha Heights (TECOM) district. It's an easy area to reach and sits right in the middle of the city's tech and education quarter.

### How do you get there by public transport?

The Dubai Metro Red Line stops at Dubai Internet City station a short ride from downtown, and the Dubai Tram links the same area to Dubai Marina and JBR. Between the metro, the tram, and taxis, the Knowledge Park and Internet City area is simple to reach without a car.

### When is the best time to scratch outdoors in Dubai in summer?

Aim for early morning or the evening. July temperatures regularly climb above 40°C, so the middle of the day is brutal — but with shade, covered walkways, and the area's constant air-conditioning close by, the cooler hours are perfectly comfortable for setting up and playing.

### What gear do you need to scratch outdoors?

Very little. A battery-powered portable turntable like the Numark PT01 Scratch, a portable fader such as the Mixfader, a scratch record, and a small speaker so passers-by can hear you. No mixer, no booth, and no power outlet required — it all fits in one bag.
