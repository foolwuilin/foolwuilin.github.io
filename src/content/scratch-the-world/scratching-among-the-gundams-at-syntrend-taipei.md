Two days earlier I'd been stood in the arrivals hall at Taoyuan, records still warm from the overhead bin. Now, barely over the jet lag, I hopped the MRT into the middle of Taipei to do something gloriously silly. As I wrote that day: "Scratching with Gundam and Stormtroopers, also visit the e-sport arena of Logitech. A nerdy trip XD." Scratch the World had reached Taiwan, and its first proper city stop was going to be inside a shrine to gadgets.

## Out of the MRT and into the tech epicentre

Come up out of Zhongxiao Xinsheng Station and you feel the shift before you read a single sign. This crossroads where Civic Boulevard (市民大道) meets Bade Road (八德路) is the humming heart of Taipei's tech world — the old Guanghua Market and the Guang Hua Digital Plaza sit right here, floors of stalls selling cables, cameras, drones and every gadget imaginable. A little to the west is Huashan 1914 Creative Park, a beautifully repurposed old sake winery where Taipei's officially licensed buskers play acoustic sets and do acrobatics in the open air. I wasn't there for the indie-folk scene, though. My shoulder bag held a battery-powered turntable, a fader and a stack of 7-inch scratch records, and my target was the glass tower rising across the traffic: Syntrend Creative Park (三創生活園區).

## Taipei's cathedral of gadgets

Step through the doors and the contrast is a slap — a wall of crisp, icy air-conditioning after the subtropical humidity outside, a genuine godsend in a Taiwanese summer. Neon highlights slide across the polished floors, and the ambient noise is a very specific blend: booming cinematic trailers from the display screens, the click-clack of a hundred mechanical keyboards, and the quiet hum of the central escalator atrium that spirals up through the building like the spine of a mothership. Syntrend is famous for its giant ground-floor pop-ups, and that day I walked in past a life-size Unicorn Gundam and a phalanx of Star Wars Stormtroopers standing guard over the latest collectibles. It's less a mall than a temple to the newest tech there is — which is exactly why it felt so funny to carry in the oldest DJ tech there is.

## Setting up by the e-sports arena

I rode the escalators up toward the gaming floors, past the colossal ROG flagship store, and found a quiet pocket of space near the Logitech G X ROG Esports Arena, not far from a few smaller independent vendor stalls. I balanced the PT01 on a sleek display ledge, dropped the needle, and for about ten minutes I traded fast fader clicks with the arena's ambient roar of soundbars and match commentary. A few teenagers slowed down to film the spinning vinyl on their phones, clearly tickled by a bit of analog tech thriving in the middle of Taipei's digital cathedral.

## Cutting a scratch completely off the books

None of this was strictly allowed, and that's worth being honest about. Street performance in Taiwan is a licensed activity — since the early 2000s Taipei's buskers (街頭藝人) have had to audition for a permit from the city's Department of Cultural Affairs and stick to designated parks, plazas and MRT stations. A private shopping mall like Syntrend sits entirely off that map, which makes a turntable on a display ledge pure guerrilla work. There's a real etiquette to pulling it off in a Taiwanese mall: keep the little internal speaker at a moderate volume so the cuts blend into the gaming soundbars rather than cutting over them; never block the flow of that escalator atrium, or security will end your set on the spot; and treat the whole thing like a casual tech demo rather than a formal show. If a guard does drift over, the respected way out is a quick bow, a soft "bù hǎoyìsi" (excuse me / sorry), and packing up smoothly — Taiwanese mall security is famously polite, and the trick is to be just as polite back.

## Why a gadget mall was the perfect Taiwan stop

Scratch the World has always been about carrying a turntable into ordinary, unexpected, in-between places and leaving a little sound behind. A building devoted to the very newest gadgets — wall-to-wall with Gundams, gaming rigs and 4K everything — is about the funniest possible stage for a battery-powered record player, and that clash is exactly why I loved it. Everything still fit in one bag I could sling over a shoulder and carry up an escalator, and that portability is the whole point. The map had reached Taipei, and it was only just getting warmed up.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you DJ or busk inside Syntrend Creative Park?

Not officially. Street performance in Taiwan is licensed — buskers (街頭藝人) audition for a permit from the city's cultural affairs department and perform only in designated public zones — and an indoor mall like Syntrend is private property, so it falls outside all of that. The only realistic way to do anything like this is quietly and briefly: keep the volume low, stay out of the walkways, and pack up the moment anyone official asks.

### What is Syntrend Creative Park (三創)?

Syntrend Creative Park (三創生活園區) is a multi-storey technology and lifestyle mall in Taipei's Guanghua digital district, right by Zhongxiao Xinsheng MRT and across from the Guang Hua Digital Plaza. It's known for its gadget and computer shops, the Logitech G X ROG Esports Arena on the gaming floors, and huge ground-floor pop-up installations like life-size Gundam statues and Star Wars displays.

### Is it hard to scratch on a portable turntable in a place like that?

The hard part isn't the crowd, it's the gear and the volume. A battery portable like the Numark PT01 has a light platter and a light tonearm, so aggressive cuts can knock the needle out of the groove, and you have to keep the internal speaker quiet enough to stay under the radar. You end up scratching smaller, cleaner and more carefully than you would on a full-size deck.

### What gear did you use?

A Numark PT01 Scratch — a battery-powered portable turntable built for scratch DJs — with a portable fader and a scratch record. Because it runs on batteries and fits in a shoulder bag, it sets up anywhere, which is the only reason a session inside a Taipei mall is even possible.
