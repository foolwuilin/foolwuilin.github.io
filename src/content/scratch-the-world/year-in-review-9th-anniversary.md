Every year I do something I love: I look back at all the places I dragged my little turntable to, and I stitch them into one video. This is year nine — and honestly, it's the fullest one yet. From 24 June 2025 to 23 June 2026 I scratched at around 60 spots across four countries, and I put every one of them into the short you can watch right up top. Let me walk you through the year.

## What "Scratch the World" means

Quick catch-up if you're new here. Scratch the World is my ongoing travel diary as a portablist — a DJ who scratches on a small, battery-powered portable turntable instead of full-size decks. Wherever I travel, I set up somewhere memorable, cut a few bars, and film it. A tourist takes a photo to mark the spot; I leave a scratch instead. Nine years in, it's become the thread that ties all my trips together.

## This past year, by the numbers

Here's what made year nine special: roughly **60 different spots**, packed into a single 12-month stretch (24 June 2025 – 23 June 2026), across **four countries** — Taiwan, Japan, China, and the UAE. Some were world-famous landmarks, plenty were ordinary street corners that just felt right. The video up top is the whole run compressed into one reel, so you can travel the year with me in under a minute.

## Where I took my turntable this year

### Taiwan (home base)

Taiwan is where it all starts and ends for me, so it has the most stops by far — too many to list. Two you'll spot right away are **Taipei 101**, cutting under the tower that defines the city's skyline, and **Ximending**, Taipei's buzzing pedestrian district where street culture and crowds make the perfect backdrop for a scratch.

### Japan

Japan gave me two of my favourite moments of the year. One was scratching by **Tokyo Tower**, the classic red-and-white landmark. The other was **Tokyo Dome** during the **2026 World Baseball Classic** in March 2026 — the Dome hosted Pool C, and my own Chinese Taipei team played there, so getting to scratch around that energy was unreal.

### China

China was the big road trip of the year. I hit a string of cities — **Shenzhen, Shanghai, Chengdu, Nanjing, and Wuhan**, among others — each with a completely different pace and feel. Moving city to city like that, turntable in my bag, was the part of the year that reminded me how much a place changes the way a scratch feels.

### United Arab Emirates

The furthest stop was **Dubai**, where I scratched with the sail-shaped **Burj Al Arab** rising behind me at Madinat Jumeirah. Even in the heat, the sea breeze kept it calm and the whole city felt easy and welcoming. I wrote that trip up on its own — you can read the full story in [Scratching by the Burj Al Arab in Dubai](/scratch-the-world/burj-al-arab-dubai/).

## Why I scratch the world, every year

People ask why I keep doing this, and the honest answer is that it's how I reset. Travel is my way to release stress — the same thing so many travellers describe: stepping into an unfamiliar city, feeling its energy, and coming home lighter than you left. Scratching just makes it mine. Instead of only seeing a place, I get to *play* in it, leave a little sound behind, and keep a record I can look back on. Nine years of these recaps now sit like a map of where the music has taken me — and this year added the most pins yet.

Over nine years of Scratch the World I've brought a portable turntable to cities across Taiwan, Japan, China, the United States, and the UAE. ### What gear do you travel and scratch with?

The same compact kit every trip: a Numark PT01 Scratch — a battery-powered portable turntable made for scratch DJs — with an Audio Innovate Mini Innofader swapped in for real fader cuts. No booth, mixer, or power outlet needed. It fits in a bag, which is the whole point of portablism and the reason I can set up almost anywhere.

### How long have you been doing Scratch the World?

This is my ninth year. What began as a way to keep scratching while I travelled turned into a yearly ritual — visiting new places, filming a session at each, and compiling them into an annual recap like this one.
