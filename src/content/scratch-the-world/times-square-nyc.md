Can I tell you about the night I scratched in Times Square? Because if you've ever dreamed of standing on that famous corner — and let's be honest, who hasn't — I think you'd love doing exactly what I did. Just before midnight on 30 December 2017, I unfolded a little portable turntable, dropped the needle, and cut a few bars right there under the lights. Here's why you should try it too.

## Why you'll want to scratch here

Times Square is a bucket-list place — the kind of spot most people want to see at least once in their life. So instead of just taking the photo everyone takes, I figured: why not leave a little piece of myself here instead? If you're into portablism, trust me, the real pull isn't a big stage — it's the story. And this is about the best story you can hand yourself.

## It's bright as day, even at midnight

Here's the thing nobody warns you about: it's nearly midnight, and the whole place still glows like noon. Those giant screens and endless ads throw off so much light that the sky above Times Square never really goes dark. Scratching under all that glow, with billboards flickering behind you, feels less like a late-night set and more like performing *inside* a screen. You have to feel it to believe it.

## What to bring (my little travel rig)

The best part? You really don't need much. I brought a **Numark PT01 Scratch** — a battery-powered portable turntable made just for scratch DJs, so you're never chained to a wall. It ships with a simple on/off "scratch switch" instead of a real crossfader, so like most portablists I popped in a **Mini Innofader**: a proper, super-responsive fader you can actually cut with. And don't stress about a booth — there are loads of public tables and seats around Times Square where you can just set up and go.

## Don't worry, you won't be out there alone

If setting up in public sounds a little nerve-wracking — I totally get it — but honestly, Times Square might be the easiest place to start. It pulls in so many tourists that there are police everywhere, and even close to midnight I never once felt on edge. You can feel the good energy coming off the crowd, and you're in great company: the square is full of people doing their thing, from costumed characters posing for tips to performers of every kind. You'll fit right in.

## Why I scratch the world (and why you might too)

This is what "Scratch the World" means to me, and maybe it'll click for you too. A tourist takes a photo to hold a moment; I take a scratch. Times Square will always sound, in my memory, like cold hands, blinding light, and one clean cut that somehow sliced right through all of it. Pick a place that means something to you, and go make your own.

## Frequently asked questions

### What gear did you use to scratch in Times Square?

A Numark PT01 Scratch — a battery-powered portable turntable made for scratch DJs — with an Audio Innovate Mini Innofader dropped in to replace the stock scratch switch, so I could do real fader cuts. That's the whole kit: no booth, no mixer, and no power outlet needed. Perfect for throwing in a bag and taking anywhere.

### What is portablism?

Portablism is basically scratching with a portable turntable instead of full-size decks. It shrinks a DJ setup down to a small turntable and a fader you can carry anywhere — which is exactly why you can set up and scratch on a street corner or right in the middle of Times Square.

### Is Times Square safe at night?

From my experience, yes — really. Because it draws so many tourists, there's a heavy police presence and constant crowds, and the billboards keep it brightly lit even near midnight. It felt safe and lively, not empty or sketchy, so it's a friendly place to try something bold.

### Do you need vinyl to scratch on a portable turntable?

The PT01 plays real records (7-inch up to 12-inch), and most portablists scratch on small vinyl for the most authentic feel. The whole point of a portable rig is that you can take that feel with you wherever you go.
