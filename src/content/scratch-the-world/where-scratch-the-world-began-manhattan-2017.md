Every long journey has a first step, and this is mine. Before the Burj Al Arab, before Times Square at midnight, before a hundred other stops around the world, there was one summer afternoon in June 2017 when I set a little portable turntable down on a table in Manhattan, pressed record, and cut a few bars out in the open New York air. I didn't know it yet, but that clip was the start of *Scratch the World*. Let me take you back to where it all began.

## The day it all started

I was out in Manhattan with my friend Marc (you might know him as @talamarc), two portable turntables between us and a wireless **Mixfader** we were dying to try outdoors. We found a round stone table, laid everything out, and just started scratching — no stage, no crowd, no plan. The Mixfader's effects sounded ridiculous in the best way, and somewhere in the middle of it I thought: *why don't I do this everywhere I go?* A tourist takes a photo to remember a place. I decided I'd leave a scratch instead. This was pin number one.

## Why Manhattan is made for street scratching

If you're going to start a street-scratching habit anywhere, Manhattan is the place. In summer the city turns into an open-air stage: the heat comes straight up off the asphalt, food carts sweeten the air with honey-roasted nuts, and there's a permanent hum of energy you can practically lean on. Two spots downtown are basically outdoor amphitheaters for anyone who performs — **Washington Square Park**, where the marble arch and the fountain make a natural stage for jazz brass bands and beatboxers, and **Union Square**, whose wide southern steps pull in breakdance crews and bucket drummers all day. Setting up a turntable out there, you don't feel like an outsider. You feel like you just joined the band.

## The little rig that started it all

Here's the whole setup from that first day, and honestly it hasn't changed much since. A **Numark PT01 Scratch** — a battery-powered portable turntable made for scratch DJs — so I'm never tied to a wall. A **Mixfader**, a wireless Bluetooth crossfader you can pair to the turntable for real, responsive cuts (that day, with the effects on, was the whole reason we were out there). And a little **Bose SoundLink** speaker so people walking by could actually hear it. That's it: a table, a friend, and a bag of gear you could carry on the subway. That portability is the entire point — and it's exactly why I've been able to do this in city after city ever since.

## What it actually feels like out there

Scratching outdoors in Manhattan is nothing like a booth. There's no hiding. You're cutting against a live soundtrack of traffic, sirens, and a hundred conversations, and you learn fast to lock your rhythm in no matter what's going on around you. People slow down, a few stop, someone always asks "wait, is that a *record*?" The subway breathes warm air up through the grates, the light bounces off glass towers, and your hands are moving while the whole city moves with you. It's equal parts nerve-wracking and addictive — and once you've felt it, a bedroom practice session is never quite enough again.

## Why I scratch the world

Looking back, that messy, joyful first session on a stone table set the whole thing in motion. I realized I didn't want scratching to live only indoors — I wanted to carry it out into real places and leave a little sound behind in each one. Manhattan will always be where that clicked: my friend, a Mixfader we couldn't stop laughing about, and the first pin on a map that now stretches around the world. Pick a place that means something to you, bring whatever gear you've got, and go start your own map.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is portablism?

Portablism is scratching with a small, portable turntable instead of full-size decks. It shrinks a DJ setup down to a little turntable and a fader you can carry anywhere — which is exactly why you can set up on a street corner, a park table, or a Manhattan plaza and start cutting.

### Where do street performers and musicians play in Manhattan?

Two of the best-known public spots are Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village — with its marble arch, central fountain, jazz bands and beatboxers — and Union Square, whose broad southern steps draw breakdance crews and bucket drummers. Both are open, lively public spaces where performing outdoors feels natural.

### What is a Mixfader?

A Mixfader is a compact wireless (Bluetooth) crossfader that pairs with a portable turntable, giving you a proper, responsive fader for real scratch cuts — plus effects. It's what my friend and I were testing on that very first Manhattan session, and it's a favorite in the portablism world for taking clean cuts on the road.

### What gear do you need to scratch outdoors?

Very little: a battery-powered portable turntable (like a Numark PT01 Scratch), a portable fader (a Mixfader or a mini fader), a scratch record, and a small speaker so passers-by can hear you. No booth, no mixer, no power outlet — it all fits in a bag.
